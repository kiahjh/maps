SCOPE AND EVIDENCE STANDARD

This dossier covers the complete 47-chapter Project Gutenberg text of Lilith, its Thoreau epigraph, and its terminal literary notes. The reading proceeded through every chapter, with a second audit of unselected paragraphs for spatial, landscape, architectural, ecological, weather, water, and route language. The catalogue deliberately keeps substantial surrounding dialogue: a description can be corrected many paragraphs later, and the meaning of a place often depends on who sees it or when it is visited.

The sole substantive source is this edition of the book. The gazetteer and route record below are editorial organization of that evidence, not additional world lore. Paragraph IDs such as 16.004 identify locally indexed source blocks; they are not page numbers or numbers printed by MacDonald. Each evidence group includes its original paragraphs, a research note, a dominant evidence type, and one neighboring paragraph on each side where available. A group can contain several kinds of evidence; qualifications in its note take precedence over its broad type label.

Observed means Vane narrates an experience; it does not guarantee ordinary physical reality. Reported means a character supplies history, directions, or an account. Transition records an experienced passage between settings. World-rule includes explanations of space, time, perception, access, and spiritual states. Dream and imagined material remains separate from completed journeys. Reference covers comparisons, backstory, epigraph, and literary apparatus. Vane explicitly admits that he makes theories too quickly and that his descriptions cannot fully reproduce this world. [01.002; 03.005; 09.046]

The last chapter leaves open whether even the apparent final waking belongs to a larger dream. For working purposes, events not subsequently reclassified are recorded as narrated waking experience, with that final qualification retained. A usable map should express the strongest relationships while leaving uncertain distance, direction, identity, and state visibly unresolved. [43.005–43.045; 44.001; 47.001–47.016]

THE SHAPE OF THE WORLD THE TEXT ACTUALLY ESTABLISHES

The earthly estate is an inhabited house, lawn, garden, and home-farm setting, with an unnamed village and an old ruined church. Oxford belongs to Vane’s educational history. The estate receives no surveyed address. The father’s manuscript mentions a blue sea, but does not place the estate on a coast. Switzerland is a proposed escape in Vane’s imagination. None supplies a geographical anchor for the strange country. [01.001; 01.016–01.021; 03.043; 05.007–05.008; 08.001]

In the dry strange country the main connected landscape includes an entry heath and pine wood; a cemetery moor with the sexton’s cottage and House of Sleep; a monster-inhabited hollow separated by low hills from a wide rocky river-bed; Evil Wood between two dry river branches; a former lake basin containing the Little Ones’ fruit valley and the Bags; Mara’s cottage in sandy country; the scaur of an ancient cataract above the fork; a great forest reaching from the valley to a hot stream; the grassy plain beyond that stream; and Bulika, its gardens, walls, streets, mines, and elevated palace. These are a connected itinerary, not a complete continental outline. [02.010; 06.001–06.008; 10.005–11.017; 12.001–12.027; 15.050–16.004; 18.010–18.027; 22.004–25.019; 32.013]

The clearest large-scale landform is the cataract and fork. Looking back from the scaur, Vane sees the dry river divide into two branches as it fell, with Evil Wood between them on the distant horizon. One is the branch he crossed before entering Evil Wood; the other is the branch he later followed upward. This is explicit topological evidence. The former lake and the broader, rougher branch have additional narrative links, but the book supplies neither a surveyed fork angle nor a universally usable east/west assignment. [11.004–11.017; 16.001–16.004; 38.015; 41.022; 42.001]

The country changes after repentance, local rain, and the burial of Lilith’s severed hand. Dry river channels fill; the monster hollow becomes a clear lake; the cataract flows and divides; the sandy upper country becomes grassland threaded with waters and new forests. This is an altered state of previously visited country, not a second adjacent desert and river system. Some restoration is first seen in a dream and only later independently encountered after awakening. [39.108–40.043; 41.037–42.024; 43.005–43.011; 44.001; 45.004–45.013]

Beyond the restored upper country, an upstream journey through an increasingly magnificent forest reaches a city on the side of a solitary mountain. The text explicitly distinguishes this city from Bulika. Its river comes down from the mountain peak through the main street, water stairs, and gate. Higher still, broken source rocks and cloud steps lead to a little door that becomes a book cover as Vane finds himself in his earthly library. This final sequence must remain distinct from Bulika’s fortifications and from the earlier mirror routes. [46.001–46.035]

SPACE, PORTALS, AND OBSERVER-DEPENDENT PLACES

Raven says Vane has entered a region of seven dimensions. A tree about thirty yards to the left stands on the earthly kitchen hearth and grows up the chimney; reaching it would cross the place where someone plays the piano. A rosebush and wild hyacinths share that breakfast-room setting. The house and forest have not left one another. These are asserted co-locations across worlds, not permission to flatten the forest into a scaled architectural floor plan. [04.050–04.089]

The library closet connects with the House of Sleep on one escape, but Raven explicitly says it is no nearer to, and no farther from, the cottage than any other place. The shared book projects between libraries without proving physical adjacency. The garret mirror, pine shimmer, garden crossing, courtyard tree/fountain passage, and final book-door are separate recorded access events. Their existence does not establish a general network of stable roads or mechanically repeatable doorways. [03.040–03.042; 04.012–04.043; 07.040–07.046; 27.013–28.001; 30.010–30.016; 46.035]

Several threats depend on the traveler. Moonlight restrains the burrowing monsters, while slopes, shadows, or moonset remove that protection. The children cross among monsters without seeing them and are safe from them in that episode. Children can see into the House of Sleep beyond Vane’s candle-lit vision. The skeleton couple are in hell while Vane and Raven in the same wood are not. Lilith is in outer darkness while observers remain physically with her in Mara’s cottage. These differences belong to the evidence and cannot be represented solely by coloring regions of a conventional map. [10.005–10.016; 17.060–17.082; 39.045–39.107; 40.044–40.058; 40.106–40.120]

Even buildings change access state: Mara’s cottage has two doors during the first visit, becomes doorless after Vane leaves, and is open again on later visits. The first nocturnal battle in Evil Wood leaves no bones in daylight. The foliage hall appears as a splendid ballroom at night and remains a vegetal enclosure by day. Record one location with different states rather than silently multiplying buildings, ruins, or battlefields. [11.005–11.014; 15.050–15.100; 16.011–16.024; 38.091–38.106; 41.037]

TIME, SKY, WEATHER, AND LIGHT

Earthly and strange-world durations do not reliably agree: an appointment can have passed days ago during a seemingly brief excursion, and the earlier visit is called last week rather than last night. At the cemetery, dawn is spring, noon summer, afternoon autumn, and night winter. The care of Lilith lasts more than three months, but that stationary duration cannot be converted into travel distance. In the House of Sleep, years are not ordinary measures of experience. [04.091–04.098; 06.007; 09.007–09.012; 19.062; 42.048–42.080; 43.001–43.004]

The country has several moons whose periods and orbits Vane never discovers. Early moons appear full; another is low, copper-colored and dented. During the horse ride a moon reaches the zenith then rolls rapidly to the horizon. During the later monster crossing it follows an irregular low arc. Instructions to face a rising moon are therefore exact narrative instructions but not a safe substitute for terrestrial east. There are local sun and west references; none supplies a complete geographical north arrow. [15.001; 15.063–15.077; 18.001; 31.045–31.048; 38.016; 40.052]

Early dryness coexists with hearing hidden waters and with exceptional accessible water: Mara provides a cup; the forest-edge hot river flows; Lilith’s palace has a clear bath. Bulika is less cold at night than the sexton’s neighborhood. The leafy hall has a warm evening despite cold elsewhere. Do not impose one uniform temperature, season, or complete surface-water absence on every district. [11.004; 13.069–13.090; 15.050–15.062; 16.014; 18.017; 25.035–25.045; 27.031]

Rain first visibly wets the desert near Mara’s house after Lilith yields. The party’s next departure still passes dry country. Water restoration has stages; a single instant of universal flooding is not narrated. The children’s earlier account of water filling the garret is explicitly uncertain between dream and waking. [39.001–39.005; 39.108–39.110; 40.001–40.043]

After the true awakening, plants are legible in dim twilight without ordinary external illumination; Vane thinks light may come from them or from the observers’ eyes. Nothing casts shadow, and the heather bells can be heard. Later sunrise, insects and summer moisture accompany the restored river. Near the mountain city, lightning reveals living forms in clouds without thunder; sweet rain becomes torrents and a rainbow while the river remains crystal-clear. These sensory changes are part of the world’s appearance. [45.001–45.013; 46.005–46.024]

WATER HISTORY AND RESTORATION, WITHOUT INVENTED HYDROLOGY

Mara calls the former country the Land of Waters: living torrents fed the lake now occupied by the fruit valley, and people lived as shepherds and cultivators. Mining and foreign commerce accompanied the growth of Bulika. She says Lilith carried away at most half the waters in an egg and the remainder fled underground, still sustaining life. This is Mara’s history. The dry channels, old cataract, former lake form, and recurrent water sounds support ancient water, but the underground network is not surveyed. [12.003; 14.001–14.006; 15.036–15.049; 16.001–16.004]

The hot stream is physically inspected: warm, metallic-tasting water wells from the ground at the back of a small cave and follows a narrow side channel. It is a distinct visible watercourse. The text does not connect its source to the great cataract or identify it as either dry river branch. The grass-borne water from the wounded leopardess is another event, without a channel and ending short of Bulika; it should not automatically become a permanent named river. [18.017–18.027; 21.005–21.020; 22.001–22.003]

Vane is sent above the cataract into the sand, following progressively louder water by stopping every few yards to listen. He digs at the loudest place until damp soil appears, places Lilith’s hand there, sees a little water ooze from beneath its fingers, and fills the pit. He does not inspect a subterranean reservoir, a dam, an egg inside the hand, or a measured spring tunnel. The hand-burial site is a temporary action location, not an independently named town or final river source. [41.017–41.019; 41.037–41.038]

On waking, he predicts a fork returning water to the fruit valley and perhaps drowning monsters, with Evil Wood between as an isle. Later he hears waters behind him in the basin. After the true awakening he actually sees the monster lake, living immobilized monsters at its bottom, the flowing cataract and two branches, and the upstream river in renewed grassland. The monsters are expressly not dead. The burial leaves no sign, and the river comes from far beyond it. The upstream river eventually leads to the mountain city and the peak/source beyond. [42.001–42.002; 42.016–42.024; 45.004–45.013; 46.021–46.035]

VEGETATION, ANIMALS, AND MATERIAL PALETTE

The earthly garden includes lawn, fountain, shrubs, a creeper-covered porch, a thrush with a snail, goldfish and a Persian cat. The home-farm church has an ancient flowering hawthorn. The pine crossing is marked by tall slender fragrant trees; deeper woodland has old grotesque trunks. Heath and cemetery terrain include low plants, heather, dry vegetation, moss and granite. The single pale rose, golden-hearted prayer-flower is an exceptional manifestation at a granite block, not evidence of a widespread ornamental crop. [04.001–04.004; 05.001–05.033; 06.001–06.008; 29.010–29.012]

Evil Wood mixes unfamiliar and familiar forms, including a smooth-stemmed eucalyptus-like tree, blade-like leaves and skull-like calyces. Nocturnal animal shapes and skeleton horses must remain distinct from day fauna. In the fruit valley, small rosy apples, plums, berries, quince and beech coexist with coarse large fruits, tubers and bulbs used by the Bags. Later orchard testimony explicitly names pears, figs, mesples and peaches. The social transformation from Little Ones into Bags affects the population of the same country; it does not imply migration to another continent. [11.005–11.014; 12.001–13.125; 33.001–33.009]

The approach to the guide forest includes tamarisk, juniper and stunted firs, then large pines, flowering shrubs, immense rose trees, vines and ivy. Inside much of the baby/nest forest, large trees stand with little shrub undergrowth; the children use high branches, one enormous walnut and bird-learned nests. Stone is scarce there. Some later local woods seem flowerless for lack of water, while the earlier hall has abundant roses: preserve that variation. Clothing is made from fibrous prickly-pear-like plant remains, aloe-like material and tough evergreen leaves, and aloe spikes become weapons. Resemblance language does not always warrant exact botanical naming. [16.005–16.014; 19.007–19.026; 32.013–32.035; 33.001–33.025; 34.007–34.012]

Observed forest animals include snake, doe and calf, bear-like cubs, miniature adult horses, dwarf cattle and small elephants; birds, owl, mice and butterflies occur around the vegetal hall. The children befriend small animals. Full-sized horses appear later at the forest outskirts. Cats, spotted and spotless leopardesses, dove and Raven have transforming or protective roles; not every appearance indicates a stable breeding habitat. The reported six-foot leech is Lilith’s account and remains doubtful. Monster fauna are many singular hybrid forms rather than a normal species distribution. [16.011–16.024; 18.010–18.018; 19.010–19.026; 33.010–33.025; 34.007–34.012; 40.044–40.058]

Bulika’s exterior gardens contain mostly unfamiliar vegetables and lack observed flowers, water and animals on that visit. Its fabric includes paving stone, walls and battlements, gravel refuse, cellar excavations, palace black and white marble, alabaster, ivory, silver and restrained-animal hardware. Its court has poplar-like stems and one taller palm-like center tree; do not substitute literal botanical species or a borrowed architectural style for Vane’s analogies. The renewed upper country gains flowering undergrowth, songbirds, rivulets, softer sweeter grass and a greater canopy with squirrels and friendly birds. No monkeys are observed there. [22.004; 23.012–23.030; 25.001–25.045; 27.013–27.018; 45.011–45.013]

Cloud forms above the mountain include horses, elephants, lions, dogs, cranes, flamingoes, pigeons, peacocks, hummingbirds, butterflies and transformed creeping life. They are visionary cloud manifestations, not coordinates for ground habitats. The final water-stair uses porphyry and serpentine; the river has a pale beryl-like clearness and flows over grass with primroses, daisies, crocuses, narcissi, pimpernels and anemones. The gate’s precious stones are described as living originals of earthly gems. [46.005–46.032]

WHAT A FUTURE MAP CAN AND CANNOT CLAIM

Safe commitments: preserve the two branches with Evil Wood between them; keep the cataract above the fork; retain the old lake basin/fruit valley relationship; connect the valley to the hot stream through the great baby-finding forest; put the grassy plain across the hot stream toward Bulika; give Bulika surrounding gardens, a refuse belt, multiple gates, a central square and an uphill palace reached by a lane; distinguish the mountain city from Bulika; show its water descending through the main street and gate, with walking steps on both sides.

Qualified commitments: Mara lies on Vane’s repeated approach between the cottage/monster country and the cataract route, but her precise position within the branch system is not triangulated. Entry pine wood and later great forest cannot be merged solely because both contain pines. The rose/ivy hall and carriage are close together and recognized on return; the broad forest reaches the hot stream, but its exact outline and the arrangement of every local grove are unknown. Several local left/right statements belong to a traveler’s viewpoint rather than global compass directions.

Keep alternatives visible for incomplete restoration outcomes. Vane anticipates re-submergence of the orchard lake and an isle of Evil Wood; the later narrated journey directly verifies the monster lake and cataract system, but does not walk the whole former fruit valley to survey its new shore. A complete restored coastline around the wood would therefore need an explicitly inferred treatment rather than an assertion that MacDonald describes its shape. [42.001; 45.004–45.013]

No whole-world scale is recoverable. Local figures include the thirty-yard overlapping tree, the hot stream only a few yards wide, the carriage less than ten paces from the hall, the hundred-foot earthly fountain jet, the sleeping spot a few hundred yards outside Bulika, and periodic listening every few yards. The proposed three-yard no-plough buffer was never realized. Vane’s horse only seemed nineteen hands high. Journey durations vary with moons, supernatural transport, long rests and changing time; they are not a conversion table for miles. [04.056; 05.008; 17.001; 18.017; 22.010; 28.001; 31.005; 41.017]

Do not invent a continent outline, an ocean boundary for the dry country, ranges of named mountains, fixed national borders, the location of other trading towns, city street names, a complete mining network, the number of gates, the exact palace dimensions, a port, or engineering that explains the magical waters. The first horizon mountains, later low hills, hot-stream rocky hills and final solitary mountain have different narrative roles; no text establishes that they are one continuous range.

Dream-only additions require their own label: snowy peak, glacier, dream garden, empty crypt, bottomless grave-mouth pit and apparent Earth return in chapter XLIII. Some dream waters are later corroborated, but that does not retroactively validate every dream route. Paradise, Uranus, Switzerland, Vallombrosa, Aladdin’s cave, Hybla, Hymettus, Indian resemblance, Maelstrom and the Thoreau farm belong to reference or reported other settings, not unmarked landmarks in the principal country. [04.006–04.010; 06.002; 07.032; 16.013; 25.001; 25.045; 43.001–44.001; 45.004]

PLACE-BY-PLACE GAZETTEER

Earthly estate, lawn, garden, and home-farm
State: Earth
Source locators: 01.001; 01.003; 04.004; 05.007; 28.001; 29.010

An old inherited estate without stated address. The ancient house has been enlarged into a rambling collection of wings and rooms. The lawn has a central fountain built by Vane’s father; its jet rises a hundred feet before foaming outward. A garden porch covered in creepers leads to a grassy nook between two parts of the house. Ground-floor windows admit Raven. Shrubs, goldfish, a thrush and the Persian cat establish ordinary garden life.

The home-farm contains old church ruins and an ancient hawthorn. Vane intends a rose wilderness around those ruins and a three-yard plough exclusion around the tree, but this is an unfulfilled plan. An unnamed village supplies the butler’s ghost-story source. The blue sea in the father’s writing and Vane’s Oxford history do not locate the estate.

Library and masked book closet
State: Earth / cross-world access
Source locators: 01.003; 01.009; 07.040; 07.046; 07.048; 07.050; 29.060; 30.011

The library occupies most of the ground floor. A large principal room has shelves nearly to the ceiling; adjoining irregular rooms connect through doors, arches, short passages and changes of level. An ancestral portrait occupies a niche among books; other portraits hang in a gallery and great rooms. One window looks over the lawn fountain.

A low, narrow, thick door is disguised by projecting frame and false book backs. Behind it is a rare-book closet with no ordinary second exit; a fixed fragment of manuscript seems impossible to remove. A dark corner contains drawers. A small eastern window and mirror throw morning sun toward the masked door; Vane reads in a western window. A couch is used for overnight sleep.

Escape from the House of Sleep places Vane at this door holding its handle. Lilith is later locked in the closet behind a marked threshold. Raven denies that this makes the closet geographically adjacent to the sexton’s cottage: the book links settings without establishing ordinary distance.

Garret, wooden chamber, and mirror apparatus
State: Earth / portal
Source locators: 02.003; 02.008; 08.044; 08.055; 09.001; 30.029

The detailed ascent runs from library to hall, great stair, first-floor main rooms, wide corridor, narrower stair, second-floor passages, a door and winding wooden stair. The huge garret has beams, rafters, long dim views, other doors, few cobwebbed windows and dusky skylights. The father’s account adds gaps without flooring where he steps joist to joist, with light entering along roof-slab edges.

A rough unpainted plank enclosure stands within the garret; its chamber narrows upward through the roof. The dusty oblong floor mirror leans against the wall, framed in ebony, with eagle ornament, chains and black ball. Another mirror stands opposite. Chains adjust a rotating hood/top, altering sunlight and reflected rays. The vista clears to heath and distant mountains before passage. Noon adjustment works in narrated entries, but Vane’s optical explanation is conjectural.

Sir Upward’s lost tower
State: Historical account
Source locators: 08.019; 08.029

Within the father’s manuscript, Raven describes Sir Upward’s library of a hundred manuscripts. From the great hall a steep rough descent and underground passage lead to a newly built tower, its stair, and top room. A tiny key opens a very large lock. The threshold reveals immense space, blue mountains and a paler blue sky. The tower subsequently disappears; it is not an extant building on Vane’s present estate.

Mirror-entry heath and first horizon
State: Dry country / entry
Source locators: 02.009; 03.001; 03.040; 09.007; 09.029

From the mirror: flat melancholy moor near at hand, low desolate broken heathy hills in the middle distance, and mountain tops on the far horizon. Once through, there is no familiar house or visible door behind, only ambiguous foglike distance. Low plants and wind are perceptible. Raven has a shadow although no shining sun is seen in the opening episode.

A later search traverses thickets and several small fir woods without finding the expected pine forest. Near-horizon sun brings winter cold. Raven points toward a blinding setting sun before the yellow light-creature leads Vane, supporting a westward initial direction in that particular scene. The placement of the mirror horizon mountains relative to later regions is not resolved.

First pine wood, shimmer, and overlapping forest
State: Dry country / cross-world overlap
Source locators: 03.040; 03.042; 04.050; 04.056; 04.064; 05.001

Tall slender fragrant pines hold twilight gloom. A shimmer between two trunks resembles heat haze, disappears near at hand, and gives uncertain sight of trees beyond. Passing it returns Vane to the wooden mirror chamber. A separate garden-following episode also ends in a pine forest, but the exact identity of each stretch is not surveyed.

Raven’s seven-dimensional explanation places a large tree about thirty yards left on the kitchen hearth, a piano-player across the path to it, a rosebush beside her, and hyacinths among the strings. As the party proceeds, trees grow larger, older and more grotesque before thinning into open heath. These are co-located perceptions, not a conventional forest encircling the house at measured setbacks.

Ruined church and prayer-flower
State: Earth / overlapping sacred setting
Source locators: 05.001; 05.007; 05.009; 05.025; 05.033

The ancient white-flowering hawthorn in the home-farm church ruins looks to Vane like a white-headed man. Raven says old worshippers continue to gather and sing; windlike music and an ascending white pigeon belong to the encounter. The pigeon interpreted as prayer and celestial stair language need no separate literal staircase.

At the foot of a granite block stands one unfamiliar anemone-like flower, pale rose with golden center and remarkable scent. Raven identifies it with prayer. It is a localized exceptional flower, distinct from the proposed rose garden that Vane has not planted.

Rocky moor and unenclosed cemetery
State: Dry country / renewed departure
Source locators: 06.001; 06.008; 07.015; 07.020; 40.059; 42.016; 45.001

Rocky moor, dry vegetation and moss lead to the cottage, first visible very far away. Gray clouds, sinking western sun, strong cold and a gray starless horizon produce the approach. The whole flat unenclosed heath is described as cemetery, although Vane sees neither marked graves nor a church. Many sleepers lie above ground like thick fallen petals; few are buried underneath.

Its apparent boundary is the horizon, not a measured wall. The sexton’s day cycles through four seasons. Later a distant light, Adam’s doorway candle, or Eve’s window candle guides the returning party. After awakening, the same heath has visible heather bells, shrubs and grass in a twilight without ordinary shadows.

Sexton’s cottage
State: Dry country / House of Sleep entrance
Source locators: 06.009; 06.045; 06.071; 40.059; 41.014; 42.021; 44.075

A small cottage with central deal table, candle, wall cupboard of bread and wine, and an inner door resembling a coffin lid. Eve stands before that door at the first arrival. A later visit specifies a clay floor. Doorway and window lights guide travelers; animals shelter or wait outside.

This is not an ordinary inn where Vane may stay awake overnight as a halfway guest. Passing the inner threshold enters the immense cold sleeping room. On his last solitary return the front room is empty, but the inner coffin-door moves. Horse and elephants wait by the outer door on the final departure.

House of Death / House of Sleep / great chamber
State: Persistent place, changing occupants
Source locators: 07.001; 07.010; 40.083; 40.106; 40.121; 40.157; 42.068; 44.054

An immense still room entered through the cottage’s inner door, with ice-like cold and pure air. Innumerable separate couches form aisles, heads toward the passages, white sheets over sleepers. Moonlight enters wall openings and gives a bluish frozen appearance. Vane cannot grasp its total outline: cathedral nave and huge barn are alternate impressions. Mara calls it the biggest room in the world, with future little nests, but no measurable dimensions follow.

Three empty couches lie by a woman with a wounded hand and a middle-aged man, later identified as Vane’s parents. Lona takes the middle empty couch, Lilith the couch to her right; Vane eventually sleeps beside his mother and Lona. The explicit local family sequence is father–mother–Vane–Lona–Lilith, or its mirror reversal. No cardinal axis is supplied.

Children share occupied women’s couches and those of Vane’s parents rather than inhabiting a separate new nursery wing. Two boys lie on either side of Lilith; the rescued white leopardess is laid across her feet, forepaws outstretched and head between them. At the true awakening many children rise, but Peter, Crispy, Lilith and the white cat remain sleeping. The parents have already departed, though a dreaming Vane earlier thought everyone gone.

The Shadow cannot enter or hurt those within. Lilith and the Shadow are expected to wake last. Low horizontal crypt-like windows are first specified in the dream excursion, so retain that qualification if illustrating the waking room. Lona simultaneously seems to emerge and is already asleep on her couch: the text explicitly resists treating every apparition as bodily displacement.

Bad Burrow / evil hollow / monster basin
State: Dry basin → restored lake
Source locators: 10.005; 10.012; 11.001; 31.045; 40.044; 40.050; 42.016; 45.004

Beyond heather the ground becomes bare, spongy and powdery like dry peat. It ripples and heaves under monsters: a tiger-like burrower, white-maned red-necked bear-headed worm, feathered serpent and many unique mixed forms. Later encounters add bodiless heads, fleshy hairy tubes, beaks, claws, tentacles and long-necked corpse-headed shapes. This is ground behaving like a sea, not an actual early sea.

Moonlight restrains the creatures; mist, hill shadows, darkness and moonset intensify danger. A low hill range and ridge offer escape. The later party crosses a shallow basin with firm ground on the far side, but children do not see the monsters. After restoration it is a clear lake with greenish depths. A whirlpool has swept away burrowing soil; the monsters lie knotted, coiled or piled motionless at the bottom, expressly alive. No surveyed shoreline is provided.

Low hills, escape ridge, and first river approach
State: Dry / restored route
Source locators: 10.015; 10.017; 11.001; 40.044; 43.009; 45.008

The low range beyond the monster country offers a moonlit ridge and a warmer short descent where Vane waits for dawn. From the summit at sunrise he sees the apparently lifeless hollow behind and an empty expanse with a differently colored possible forest ahead. Descent reaches the first exposed river-bed. Later Mara’s party reaches a ridge above the basin at sunset after crossing the river channels.

The book establishes a separating ridge and journey order, not a named mountain chain or elevation. It does not identify these hills with the first mirror horizon mountains, the hot-stream source hills, or the final solitary mountain.

First dry river branch / broader, rougher branch
State: Dry channels → flowing water
Source locators: 11.001; 11.004; 16.004; 31.045; 38.015; 41.022; 45.008

The first immense crossing before Evil Wood consists of exposed rock, many water-runs, flat or channelled surfaces, humped and pinnacled places, dry moss and hard lichens. A day passes before Vane reaches the wood. Sounds suggest water but he crosses no actual water then. Night riding later involves ravines and chasms, and the horse falls by a wide shallow channel near the midst of the crossings.

The cataract view recognizes this as one of the two branches. Later Vane wants help crossing the broader, rougher branch on the route toward the cemetery; Mara supplies an easy crossing. On the hand mission he crosses channels of the first branch and judges Mara near. Those passages connect the route but do not specify all individual channels or a compass bearing.

Evil Wood
State: Nocturnal phantoms / anticipated restored isle
Source locators: 09.024; 11.005; 11.009; 11.014; 16.004; 32.004; 42.001

A mixed forest unlike the entry pine wood, with glades, enclosing low boughs and unfamiliar eucalyptus-like growth. Foliage suggests wolves, horses, skeletons and a woman as cold and wind arrive. At night Vane sees a violent phantom/skeleton battle and misty or white-boned horses. Just before sunrise the figures collapse; daylight reveals no bones, only withered branches and burial-like sounds. No ordinary birds or small animals are seen on that day.

The scaur view explicitly places the wood between the two river branches. Raven says Vane’s grandfather is among those who fight there instead of sleeping. Cats later drive Vane through the wood unseen on his way back to the fruit valley. Restoration would make it an isle in Vane’s anticipation; the later party does not survey its full new water boundary.

Second dry river branch and upward bank route
State: Dry → restored
Source locators: 11.015; 12.001; 14.025; 15.068; 16.004

On emerging from Evil Wood, Vane crosses another network of dry channels. He initially fears he has reversed direction, then supposes another branch; the later scaur view confirms two branches. A broad mossy channel offers a sleeping place, and he hears water with his head to the ground. Shrub-like vegetation and dwarf fruit trees increase toward the former lake basin.

After leaving the children he travels up this branch, whose bank eventually leads toward Mara and the old cataract route. The return from the nest village crosses a branch described as left from that traveler’s viewpoint. No text fixes all these local left/right words to a universal north-up orientation.

Former lake basin / fruit valley / Little Ones’ hollow
State: Dry habitation / restoration anticipated
Source locators: 12.001; 12.003; 13.001; 13.069; 14.001; 15.039; 33.001; 42.001

A hollow interpreted as an old lake basin. Forest flows into it from the left; grass and moss cover descending slopes. Fruit trees dominate, larger coarse-fruited growth at the rim and tiny rosy apple trees among them. Tiny trees fill intervals like brushwood. The small fruit nourishes children and Vane; giant food includes large unpalatable green apples, tubers and bulbs.

Children hide among small growth and warm Vane at night; Lona brings babies from the wood. They have no ordinary counting of age and have never seen water until tears, though the region shows ancient water and offers hidden-water sounds. Their country is bound up with identity. Mara later confirms the receiving-lake history. Vane expects the lake to refill; the final shoreline and the survival or relocation of every orchard tree are not narrated.

Bags’ huts and changing occupation of the valley
State: Dry country / before and after relocation
Source locators: 12.024; 13.001; 13.110; 33.001; 33.026

The initial giant settlement is in wood above the valley. Huts use fallen branches and a few stones; Vane lies on bare ground at a hut door and is tied to a fruitless work tree by day. Lona reports a largest unseen giant worshipped in one house. This reported house is not a visited landmark with a known plan.

The Bags destroy small fruit trees after Vane leaves. The Little Ones move beyond the giants’ range. Over a hundred giants later attack the nest forest and are repelled from fifty trees with collected stones and elephants. They depart to the other end of the orchard valley and never return to that forest. The old huts and the later giant location belong to different stages; no permanent administrative border is established.

Great baby-finding forest between valley and hot stream
State: Dry country / changing community
Source locators: 13.020; 14.021; 16.006; 30.003; 32.013; 33.001

Vane eventually explicitly identifies a forest extending all the way from the valley to the hot stream. Earlier resemblance of the guide forest to the baby forest is only his conjecture; the later statement establishes the broad connection. Parts are open, with large trees and little shrub undergrowth. Children search it at night for infants.

The source of all infants is unresolved: children guess tree-drop or eggs, and Vane later guesses mothers from Bulika. One fugitive mother does reach the colony, so that route is real, but it is not a universal explanation. Adam gives Lona a distinct history of divine fostering in the wilderness. No single nursery, egg grove, or portal-origin point can be mapped as fact.

Little Ones’ high nest village
State: Dry country / later colony
Source locators: 32.024; 32.033; 33.001; 33.019; 34.007; 38.014

After giant damage, the children build sleeping nests high in tall welcoming trees, beyond the Bags’ range but close enough for nocturnal fruit visits to the old orchards. They learn nests from birds and climb bough to bough; they have arms and legs, not wings. One occupied tree is an enormous walnut. There is little underbrush and no conventional street of houses.

Stone is scarce, gathered from far away for nest defense. Lona’s tree is the site of the spotted and white cats’ battle. Bright birds, friendly miniature animals, stored fruit, later full-sized horses on the outskirts and leaf garments give this site its specific appearance. The party returns here after the ivy hall, stays a night and resettles most children before the smaller group goes to Mara. No precise forest area or nest count follows from the fifty defended trees.

Mara’s sandy approach, ravines, and hollows
State: Dry country / transition
Source locators: 14.025; 15.001; 15.035; 38.015; 40.043

Leaving the children’s branch escort brings Vane into sandy country. Numerous small ravines obstruct his night walk; he sleeps in a sandy hollow. Mara’s cottage appears in alignment with a setting moon, light shining through an open door or window. This is a sightline, not a building on or near the lunar surface.

Later the small returning party camps in desert between the branches and sees the house before evening on the next day. The route confirms a sandy inter-branch approach, but does not triangulate the cottage. Local rain later arrives while surrounding travel country is still dry.

Mara’s House of Bitterness
State: Dry / repentance / first local rain
Source locators: 15.050; 15.062; 15.085; 38.091; 39.006; 39.108; 41.037

A cottage resembling the sexton’s but with thicker walls, its rock foundation deep in a wide tract of sand. A heavy strong outer door leads directly into a large bare room. Two small opposite glassless windows and two doors produce the original moon sightline. A horn lantern stands at the hearth. Mara lives alone except for guests.

A narrow steep stair leads to an unpartitioned, unceiled garret with one broad low dormer. The guest bed stands under the slope with a white coverlet; dry bread and cold water are supplied. On leaving, Vane finds the house abruptly doorless and cannot get back in. Later visits restore the open doorway.

The main room later has clay floor, small hearth fire, a lamp high on the chimney-piece and a rough settle initially at one side. It is moved to the center with Lilith’s feet toward the hearth; Mara and Vane sit on opposite sides of the fire. Mara’s own bed lies in a downstairs corner, separate from the guest bed above. Bread and pitcher remain on the table during the two later empty-house passages.

Cats, inner fire, invisible water and Lilith’s inward hell belong to its changing experiential state. The children cannot say whether their garret flood and cats were dream or waking. Actual soft rain wets the outer desert at dawn after Lilith’s surrender; this is separately narrated.

Old cataract scaur / great restored cataract
State: Dry rock → flowing fork
Source locators: 16.001; 16.004; 41.017; 41.038; 45.010

After many unspecified desert miles, Vane reaches and climbs a rock marked by an ancient waterfall, with green moss and audible hidden water below. Above lies fine sand filling an upper river course. Ahead and left desert stretches beyond sight; a far-right forest skyline gives his next direction. Looking back reveals the river dividing as it fell, and Evil Wood between the two branches far away.

After restoration, the party follows the river upstream along its right bank without crossing to the sheer cataract at the foot of the former sandy desert. Above the climb is a broad deep quiet brim-full river in grassland. The text describes a shared cataract and two branches, not two separate waterfall sources.

Upper sand and the burial of Lilith’s hand
State: Dry action site → erased in restoration
Source locators: 16.003; 41.017; 41.038; 42.001; 45.011

Above the cataract stretches a sandy upper plain and old river course. Adam sends Vane deep into it, following underground sound by repeated listening every few yards. At the loudest point he digs a large pit through loose sand to damp soil. Water oozes from under the hand’s fingers before he covers it level with the desert and sleeps.

The waking ground is moist and his old track has quicksand as the river stirs. On the final upstream passage there is no sign of the burial, and the river comes from far beyond. This place begins the narrated release without becoming the ultimate river headwater. Its exact distance or direction from the scaur is not measured.

Guide forest and rose wood
State: Dry country / route to the hot stream
Source locators: 15.068; 16.005; 16.010; 16.025; 18.001

Mara instructs Vane to go up the river bank until it disappears, verge right to forest, then leave it facing a rising moon and keep that heading. Tamarisk and juniper give way to stunted firs, larger firs, and pines mixed with other trees. Large trunks stand almost geometrically, with little undergrowth, long views, colored and white butterflies and some birds.

The pines open into flowering shrubs and splendid tree-sized roses among immense columnar trees. The hall and carriage occur within this route. Later recognition on the return from Bulika places them between the hot-stream crossing and the nest village, but no measured forest path or perimeter is supplied.

Rose-and-ivy vegetal hall
State: Vegetal enclosure / nocturnal ballroom
Source locators: 16.011; 16.024; 18.003; 38.008; 38.009

A house or castle-like shape appears among plants, but Vane cannot verify masonry; an ivy-swallowed ruin is his conjecture. A rectangular doorless gap opens into hall-like space with grass and flowers for floor and ivy, vine and roses for walls and roof. He beds on dead leaves in a corner. Sky and stars show through the branch roof; nesting birds, owl and mice inhabit it. Grapes grow by its doorway.

At midnight the place seems a splendid hall with ancient dancers whose faces are skulls and whose bodies pass through Vane. Wind strips finery and bodies away. The returning children later play among the dancers. A Lilith-like doorway apparition does not establish a second palace or a second bodily captive. For illustration the plant fabric is secure; stone towers would be invention.

Mouldering carriage and skeleton pair’s thicket
State: Dry country / close to ivy hall
Source locators: 17.001; 17.060; 38.011

Before walking ten paces after leaving the hall Vane catches sight of a carriage and then approaches it; the text does not measure the full separation between hall and carriage. The old carriage stands upright on heavy wheels, with fallen doors, two horse skeletons by the pole, a coachman’s skeleton and two skeletal passengers. Fibrous grass binds a damaged joint; a fallen branch serves as crutch. This establishes a nearby relic site, not a road network or known carriage destination.

On return the same pair occupy a nearby thicket and move more freely, gathering herbs; Vane’s proposed explanation that they rub their bones with them is conjecture. Raven says the hall dancers are spiritually far ahead of the pair although physically close. Their hell is a co-located state, not a separate region.

Lilith’s discovery tree and converging animal tracks
State: Dry forest / temporary care site
Source locators: 18.001; 18.008; 18.010; 18.018

Facing a low copper-dark dented moon, Vane finds a wasted cold woman under a spreading tree on hard root-woven ground and pine needles. He brings grapes from the hall, makes a branch-and-leaf bed and tends her. Her initial arrival and life/death condition are not explained by a confirmed local history.

Before dawn, animal paths converge into a well-beaten track, widening as lesser tracks join. Miniature horses, dwarf cattle, elephants and other animals lead to the forest edge. The route establishes an approach to the hot river; no surveyed distance from the hall or source cave is given.

Hot river at the forest edge
State: Visible water / protective crossing
Source locators: 18.017; 18.020; 25.046; 30.004; 32.013; 38.001

A deep brim-full stream, only a few yards wide, runs along the forest edge beside a vast green grassy plain. Bluish mist rises from hot, metallic-tasting water. Its current is swift and steady. Animals swim across toward grazing; Vane supposes they sleep in the wood and feed on the plain.

This is Mara’s instructed running-stream crossing before Bulika. Adam later confirms that it stopped Lilith’s attack and that she could bypass it only through the three-dimensional world in contact with Vane. The barrier is a magical crossing restriction, not a built wall. The return party crosses it before the ivy hall and nest village; Lilith’s eyes close after crossing.

Rocky hot-stream source cave and bridge-bed
State: Temporary dwelling / inspected spring
Source locators: 18.025; 18.033; 19.001; 19.027

Upstream nearby rocky hills contain a small cave at the foot of one hill. Water rises quietly from the ground at the back, like the base of a column, and runs along one side through a deep narrow channel. The outlet does not fill the whole cave entrance. Vane’s imagined internal waterfall stair is explicitly disproved.

He arranges boulders and spans the stream with fallen beech boughs, dry yellow leaves, cross-twigs and packed leaves/moss, making a bed warmed from below. Warm water keeps air fresh but sometimes oppressive; outside air is cooler. Forest fruit and fiber supply food and clothing. Care extends beyond three months. This is a makeshift bridge-bed, not a village or permanent stone bridge.

Grassy plain between forest-edge stream and Bulika
State: Dry country / pursuit and marches
Source locators: 20.001; 20.018; 21.001; 22.004; 34.013; 35.001; 38.001

Beyond the stream grass thickens toward a far green horizon. Leaving it, Lilith crosses a gentle upslope; Vane follows across thick soft grass for a whole day. The two cats pursue the same line. A fleeing mother travels on a parallel line with an infant. No named highway, compass bearing or mile count is supplied.

Children had reported smooth green country without rocks or trees toward the moonrise side beyond their wood. The army later crosses silent levels of short grass, carrying hay because elephants cannot gather enough of the short blades. Rest and night-march stages, supernatural fauna and uncertain sky cycles prevent exact distance conversion. Outside Bulika the grass grows thicker and softer at the temporary camp.

Channel-less water from the wounded leopardess
State: Transient observed water
Source locators: 21.005; 21.020; 22.001; 22.003

A spotted leopardess wounded in the left forepaw leaves a copious flow Vane first mistakes for blood. Testing proves it water. It murmurs across grass without a channel, with force at his ankles. He follows it toward Bulika; at dawn it stops short of the walls, leaving bent grass and little pools. Its permanence and subsequent course are not established. A fixed blue river on a map would overstate this evidence.

Bulika: country, gardens, walls, gates, and streets
State: Dry city / historical Land of Waters
Source locators: 15.036; 22.004; 22.008; 22.011; 23.001; 35.007

Mara names the city in grassy country and reports its rise from pastoral and agricultural life through diamond/opal mining and foreign commerce. Lilith’s sweeping imperial claims and citizens’ assertions of her power do not establish surveyed political borders. Other manufacturing towns are unnamed and unlocated.

At dawn old wall towers stand against pale sky. Vegetable gardens closely surround walls, with mostly unfamiliar crops and no flowers, water or animals seen. A belt of great gravel/refuse heaps lies between gardens and wall, thrown from battlements. Several roads approach different gates. One half-closed unguarded gate has apparently immovable hinges; the fortifications are neglected, not actively sealed.

Within are long narrow old streets, tall houses, stone paving, small archway hiding places and nearly impassable lanes connecting wider streets. No street names, full grid, gate count or city diameter is given. The populace leaves street centers to the advancing children and later watches from doors and windows. Vane’s early sunny sleeping spot is a few hundred yards outside the walls.

Bulika’s central square and bat pillar
State: Dry city / route hub
Source locators: 22.008; 35.007; 35.014; 36.023

An early small square contains a pillar topped with a hideous bat-like figure. During the army occupation a central square serves as camp; a narrow lane directly connects it to the palace. After Lona’s death Vane rests at the pillar base in the now-empty square. The repeated route and pillar support identifying these scenes as the same square, but that identity is contextual rather than a repeated street address.

The gate-to-square-to-palace sequence is secure. Its cardinal position, the shape of the plaza, the lane length and the orientation of the bat figure are not specified.

Citizen’s stair, landing, stone room, and adjoining chamber
State: Dry city / repeated visits
Source locators: 23.031; 24.001; 24.009; 26.014

After a maze of lanes, a steep narrow stair lies between two tall houses. Its top landing, about a man’s length, separates opposite doors and shelters Vane from the street wind. One door closes against him. Mara later admits him to an empty stone-paved room where a rug serves as bed, lit by moonlight.

A narrow interior passage leads to the adjacent newborn’s room. The spotless leopardess has too little room to turn on the stair and somersaults out. Vane retraces the stair to the street; later Shadow follows the same route. The repeated woman-death scenes should be retained in their sequence rather than normalized into a rationalized floor plan or an invented second street.

Gem cellars and excavation labyrinth
State: Reported industry / observed child pit
Source locators: 23.012; 23.030; 35.016; 35.019

Citizens say they dig gems in their cellars, living on inherited wealth and trade while manufactured goods come from other towns. During the raid on one house Vane enters empty upstairs rooms and an under-stair door opening to a labyrinth of excavations, where a boy is in a hole. This physically confirms underground excavation, not the connection of every cellar beneath the entire city. A second nearby house contains a murdered child.

Elevated fortress-palace and outer defenses
State: Dry city / observed architecture
Source locators: 24.011; 24.017; 25.001; 36.008

The palace stands high over the city’s heart, approached by a steep way, with many defenses and partly ruined neglected fortifications. Great gates remain open. A drawbridge-like crossing spans a rocky ravine/chasm used as moat; Vane doubts it ever contained water. A recessed stone bench outside shelters him at night.

Inside is a paved court/grove of regularly spaced towering poplar-like stems, with one still taller center tree. The palace is long and low with high corner towers and a great central dome only half the towers’ height. A low half-elliptical central front arch enters the vestibule. Its suggested Indian origin is Vane’s analogy, not permission to import an Indian palace plan.

Courtyard’s tallest tree and fountain passage
State: Dry city / Earth transition
Source locators: 25.001; 27.013; 27.018; 27.031; 28.001; 30.004

The central court tree has branches gathering their ends into a fir-cone-like crown and trunk projections resembling old palm leaf bases. Lilith claims a healing blossom and dangerous snake at the top; Vane never verifies either. Cold increases as he climbs despite little wind and unmoving branches. Near the top support feels unstable, then he is drenched and flung into the earthly fountain basin.

Adam explains that Lilith touched Vane’s wrapped feet throughout the ascent, gaining contact needed to reenter Earth and bypass the hot-stream prohibition. The hundred-foot height is the fountain jet in the ensuing Earth scene, not a measured height of this tree.

Vestibule, restraint cage, kitchen, and irregular passages
State: Dry palace interiors
Source locators: 25.001; 25.019; 26.012

A longish elliptical vestibule has the spotted leopardess’s cage to one side, muzzle, restraining paw mitts and collar. Two low passages lead into many narrow irregular branches; a page guides Vane. A large kitchen contains servants and provisions. The exact passages, room locations, levels and correspondence to exterior corner towers remain undescribed.

A later nocturnal encounter seems to show the beast leaving yet also remaining in the cage. That double appearance must not become a second ordinary animal cage or a duplicated palace wing without explicit labeling.

Black-marble elliptical great hall
State: Dry palace / precise local geometry
Source locators: 25.019; 25.020; 26.007; 36.003; 36.016

An enormous dark chamber has close-fitting black marble floor and walls; its plan/wall is elliptical, its roof the long half of an ellipsoid. A high oval opening lies over one focus, yielding a small patch of light rather than general illumination. Later the princess’s chair is at the north focus under the aperture, on a dais, with a mirror a little above and in front of her that reflects sunlight onto her. Meridional sunlight briefly illuminates her.

This north-focus statement is unusually precise local orientation. It still gives no citywide compass, room dimensions or explicit identification of this roof with the external central dome. A black curtain and white stair lead to the guest accommodation. Nocturnal dancers, shadow bodies and monsters mingle in the dark hall; images from the princess’s brain are not additional geographical rooms.

Lona dies on its marble. Frightened horses run into the unseen black wall; elephants reach the dais. These events confirm local floor, wall and dais relations, not the total hall scale.

White stair, guest chamber, and sunken bath
State: Dry palace / private water
Source locators: 25.020; 25.032; 25.035; 26.003; 26.007

Behind a black curtain a white stair ascends to a beautiful guest chamber. A robe lies at the couch foot; a lamp hangs from the ceiling. A great white clear-water bath is sunk in the marble floor at one corner, with an unfamiliar delicate fragrance. The source and supposed enchantment are conjectural. This private water is an exception to the dry country, not proof of a public city water system.

Round alabaster dining room
State: Dry palace / downstairs interior
Source locators: 25.035; 25.045; 25.055; 26.007

At the foot of the white stair a room to the left is round, all alabaster, without windows. Light filters through the material everywhere; a low dome carries flitting shadow effects. An ivory table and couch hold bread, cakes, fruit, milk, wine and silver goblets. The princess leads Vane back upstairs afterward. The room’s immediate connection is stated; its external facade or palace position is not.

Temporary camp outside Bulika
State: After Lona’s death / captive Lilith
Source locators: 36.023; 37.001; 37.013; 37.031; 38.001

The party exits by the entry gate to children gathered in thicker softer grass. Lona’s body lies among them; Lilith is watched separately outside the camp perimeter, with sentries and elephants. A white leopardess circles at moonrise and passes between captive and children. There are no built defenses or permanent dwellings.

Children report the Great Shadow descending from the palace hill and expanding through them before they flee out of the gate. Odu’s imagined home for him in moonless night is not a verified destination. The camp’s distance from the wall is not equated to Vane’s earlier few-hundred-yard sleeping spot.

Renewed upper river, grassland, rivulets, and greater forest
State: After true awakening
Source locators: 45.008; 45.010; 45.011; 45.013; 46.001

Above the flowing cataract, the old sandy desert is grassy plain with a broad deep quiet brim-full river, swum by children and animals. Forests have flowering shrub undergrowth, songbirds and a rivulet from every thicket. The burial place has vanished; the river comes from far beyond. The party follows upstream, with sweeter grass, lovelier flowers and larger trees.

The greatest forest’s thick arched canopy scarcely admits sunshine through leaves and blossoms. Children climb into an airy vault of branches; elephants answer from below, squirrels play, birds are friendly, and no monkeys appear. The next highest-tree lookout reveals the mountain city across a plain. No text proves this greater forest is exactly the former nest wood.

Unnamed mountain city, water-street, gate, and ascent
State: Final upstream country / called heaven
Source locators: 46.001; 46.005; 46.021; 46.025; 46.032; 46.033

Far across a plain, a strange city occupies the side of a solitary mountain, summit lost in cloud; it is explicitly unlike Bulika. Rock, palace and cloud first blend visually. A tower or rock rises above the city nearer the crest, but its nature is uncertain. Lightning reveals living cloud forms without thunder. Rain intensifies and a rainbow spans the city.

The river comes visibly from the very peak through the main street. Clear pale beryl-like water descends deep broad steps of porphyry and serpentine through the gate toward the mountain foot. Shallower walking steps on both banks lead upward; the bed supports flowering grass instead of sand or shingle. The gate is open. A dark-visaged woman-angel portress stands outside on its landing; descending woman-angels receive the children.

The gate consists of living archetypes of precious stones. A great angel calls it heaven/kingdom, mentions black bats on unlocated frontiers, and orders animals to royal stables and then the King’s forest. Neither destination is visited or spatially described. The city is not named New Jerusalem in this scene, though Eve previously bears that biblical title.

At the upper edge there is no wall. Huge broken rocks like a glacier moraine let the river billow through gaps. Three or four giant steps vanish into a snow-white cloud. The throne/source is seen with the mind’s eye. Angel guidance ends before Vane climbs with hands and feet and must release Lona’s hand.

Cloud fringe, golden-lock door, and closing book
State: Final transition to Earth
Source locators: 46.033; 46.035; 47.001

After the rocky climb and cloud steps, Vane passes into white cloud folds. An unseen warm strong hand leads him to a little door with a golden lock, pushes him through, and he turns to see a large book cover closing. He is alone in the earthly library. This is the final return mechanism; neither a known route back through the mirror nor a conventional gate road. Lona is absent, Mara sometimes present, and Vane waits rather than attempting another mirror journey.

Chapter XLIII dream-only geography and false waking
State: Explicitly dream; partial later corroboration
Source locators: 43.001; 43.005; 43.031; 44.001

Sleeping Vane experiences snowy peak above a mist sea, glacier, moon and torrent, cloud-riding, renewed senses and desired gardens/houses. An apparent awakening takes him through an empty crypt-like House of Sleep and cottage, past a clear monster lake and filled river channels crossed by wading, leaping and swimming.

A bottomless grave-mouth pit in a rock space seems to lead to the earthly garret. The house appears silent and deserted; fountain and mirrors remain, but the portal will not reopen. After three nights and a fourth awakening, the narration declares all this was dreaming. The restored waters are later corroborated; the empty chamber, pit portal and false Earth stay are not thereby established as waking geography.

Off-itinerary places, comparisons, and apparatus
State: Reference / reported remote lore
Source locators: 00.001; 01.001; 03.043; 04.006; 06.002; 07.032; 16.013; 25.001; 25.045; 29.042; 45.004; 47.017

Thoreau’s epigraph describes Spaulding’s Farm in Concord, pine/oak halls, cranberry meadow and a cart-path through an unseen household: a framing analogy for overlap, not a stop on Vane’s journey. Oxford, the unnamed village and the father’s blue sea are earthly context. Switzerland is an unrealized fantasy. Raven’s rainy burrower-filled steppes of Uranus are reported remote lore.

Paradise/Noah, Vallombrosa, Aladdin, Hybla, Hymettus, Indian resemblance, Maelstrom and New Jerusalem occur as allusion, analogy, testimony or title. The terminal notes cite William Law and Dante; the final Novalis quotation is philosophical. They are all retained in the evidence index to prevent accidental conversion of evocative words into local landmarks.
